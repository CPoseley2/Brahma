# U6 Coaching Standards Research

## Practical conclusion

The official national sources reviewed do not establish a pass/fail U6 skills test. They describe a player-centered developmental environment and age-appropriate priorities. The Team Hub therefore uses observational prompts, not grades, rankings, tryout scores, or predictions.

## U.S. Soccer principles used

### Player-centered grassroots coaching

U.S. Soccer's grassroots pathway emphasizes a player-centered approach and the Play-Practice-Play methodology. Players should learn through play while the coach supports their developmental needs.

Source:
- U.S. Soccer, “Five Things to Know About the Grassroots Pathway Coaching Initiative”
  https://www.ussoccer.com/stories/2018/01/five-things-to-know-about-the-grassroots-pathway-coaching-initiative

### 4v4 environment

U.S. Soccer describes 4v4 for young grassroots players as structured, age-appropriate play without goalkeepers or offside. Fun, safety, development, and opportunity to play are more important than winning and losing.

Source:
- U.S. Soccer, “Five Things to Know About the 4v4 Online Grassroots Course”
  https://www.ussoccer.com/stories/2018/02/five-things-to-know-about-the-4v4-online-grassroots-course

## US Youth Soccer U6 developmental priorities used

The US Youth Soccer Player Development Model describes U6 as “Me and My Ball.” It emphasizes:

- General balance, coordination, agility, running, starting and stopping, jumping, hopping, and changing direction
- Dribbling, stopping and starting, and shooting
- Experimenting with a rolling ball
- Sharing, fair play, emotional management, and positive parental involvement
- Simple field concepts, including boundaries and which goal to attack
- Short attention spans, simple rules, imagination, generous praise, and individual or parallel play
- One ball per child and a player-centered environment with limited interruption
- Short sessions, with the model recommending no more than 45 minutes

Source:
- US Youth Soccer Player Development Model, U6 Fundamental Stage
  https://www.usyouthsoccer.org/wp-content/uploads/sites/160/2023/09/Player-Development-Model-Oct-2013.pdf

## Technical progression used

The US Youth Soccer Skills School Manual identifies the U6 technical progression as ball manipulation with:

- Dribbling
- Shooting
- Balance
- Running
- Jumping
- Movement education

Source:
- US Youth Soccer Skills School Manual
  https://www.usyouthsoccer.org/wp-content/uploads/sites/160/2023/09/2017-Skills-School-Manual.pdf

## App observation scale

The Team Hub uses four descriptive states:

1. Not observed
2. Exploring
3. Emerging
4. Seen in play

These are snapshots of what a coach noticed in a particular environment. They are not fixed labels, competitive scores, or comparisons with teammates.

## Parent communication design

Parents see:

- Specific celebrations
- One playful next step
- Friendly descriptions of skills seen or being explored
- Their own player’s event RSVP
- Shared team schedule and volunteer jobs

Parents do not see:

- Other families’ contact information
- Other players’ development records
- Private coach notes
- Team-wide player comparisons

## Production caution

Some official source documents currently hosted by US Youth Soccer were originally published in 2013 and 2017. They remain useful as developmental guidance, but the production app should keep source metadata and allow the club to revise its framework when national or local guidance changes.
